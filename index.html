ABSTRACT

Context. Extragalactic transients are moments of cosmic beauty, fleeting yet powerful, like a sudden burst of আলো in the vast darkness.

Aims. I introduce myself, a PhD student at the Astronomical Observatory of the University of Warsaw, and point the reader to my research, publications and observing activity.

Methods. Multi-wavelength observations of supernovae, active galactic nuclei and blazars, combined with optical follow-up through the BHTOM global telescope network.

Results. Seven refereed articles, three preprints, ten Astronomer’s Telegrams and Transient Name Server reports, about 120 hours of awarded ground-based telescope time, nine Swift target-of-opportunity triggers and talks on three continents.

Conclusions. The work is ongoing. Correspondence is welcome at the addresses above.

Key words. supernovae: general – galaxies: active – tidal disruption events (TDE) – changing-look AGN (CL-AGN) – Bowen fluorescence flares (BFF) – BL Lacertae objects: general – methods: observational – astronomers: individual: J. Majumdar

1.Introduction

নমস্কার. I am a PhD student at the Astronomical Observatory of the University of Warsaw, advised by Prof. Łukasz Wyrzykowski and co-advised by Dr. Mariusz Gromadzki. I work on multi-wavelength studies of astrophysical transients and high-energy astrophysics, and at present mostly on core-collapse supernovae that keep shining through interaction with their surroundings (Sect. 2 of the research page).

Alongside the science I help develop BHTOM, a multi-wavelength archival and optical follow-up engine for astrophysical transients. My enthusiasm after a new transient alert is empirically found to follow

E(t)∝t−5/3, (1)

the canonical fallback rate of a tidal disruption event, except that every new alert resets t to zero.
